## How this program works

This workbook turns *The Elo Blueprint* into a day-by-day schedule. Each day you open one page, do the tasks for the amount of time you have, and fill in the record box. You never have to decide what to study next.

The program covers **Stages 1–4 (Chapters 1–23)** in 90 days:

| Days | Stage | Chapters | Milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–21 | 1 · 400–600 · Stop giving things away | 1–6 | Stage 1 Test on day 21 |
| 22–49 | 2 · 600–800 · See the tactics | 7–13 | Stage 2 Test on day 49 |
| 50–70 | 3 · 800–1000 · Calculate and convert | 14–18 | Stage 3 Test on day 70 |
| 71–90 | 4 · 1000–1200 · Positional judgment | 19–23 | Stage 4 Test on day 90 |

Stage 5 (Chapters 24–28) follows as **Phase Two**, described at the end of this workbook.

Every seventh day is an **evaluation day**: no new material, a short re-test of the week's hardest exercises, a review of your journal, and an update of your weakness list. The last day of each stage is a **milestone day** with the stage test and a list of benchmarks.

:::coach An honest word about ratings
Ninety days of consistent work is enough to remove most of the mistakes that hold a 400 player down, and many players gain several hundred rating points in that time. But rating gains vary enormously between people, depend on how many games you play and against whom, and do not arrive on a schedule. Measure yourself by the benchmarks in this workbook — habits and skills — and let the rating follow. Treat any promise of a guaranteed rating jump, including from yourself, with suspicion.
:::

## Choose your daily time

Each day lists four versions of the same session. Pick one before you start and keep it for at least a week.

| Option | Typical content | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **15 min** | exercises + a little reading; games on weekends | very busy days; keeping the streak |
| **30 min** | review queue + reading + exercises | steady progress through the book |
| **60 min** | review + reading + exercises + one slow game | the recommended standard |
| **90 min** | all of the above + careful self-analysis | fastest progress |

On the 15- and 30-minute options you will not finish a chapter's reading in its allotted days. That is fine: carry the reading forward and keep the exercises on schedule. If you are consistently on 15–30 minutes, consider running the program over 180 days (two calendar days per program day).

:::cheat The daily rules
1. **Exercises first, before you are tired.** Write down your move *and* the opponent's best reply before checking.
2. **Slow games only.** 15+10 or slower online, or a correspondence/daily game. No blitz or bullet is ever required.
3. **Analyse every slow game yourself before using an engine** — even five minutes counts.
4. **Every mistake goes in the journal** with a category.
5. **Missed exercises come back** after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days (the review queue).
:::

## The record box

Each day page ends with a record box. Fill it in honestly — it is the data your weekly evaluation runs on.

* **Exercises:** how many you attempted and how many you got right on the *first try*.
* **Game:** result, time control, and the opening you played.
* **Mistakes:** tick the categories that appeared in your game or exercises.
* **One thing I learned / Tomorrow I will:** one sentence each.

If you use the interactive trainer, it records exercise results, review intervals and game analysis automatically; use the paper box for your reflections.

## Weekly evaluation and adaptation

On every evaluation day, compute your **first-try accuracy** for the week's chapter exercises and decide:

| Your result | What to do next week |
|---|---|
| ≥ 90 % and no repeated mistake category | Continue. You may compress: merge the next chapter's two study days into one. |
| 80–89 % | Continue on schedule. |
| 70–79 % | Continue, but spend the first 10 minutes of every session on this week's missed exercises. |
| < 70 % | **Repeat**: insert one consolidation day per chapter of this week before moving on (re-read the summary and cheat code, re-solve all misses, play one game focused on the skill). |

The program is a sequence, not a deadline. Inserting repeat days is part of the system, not a failure.

:::evidence Why the program is built this way
Testing yourself (retrieval practice) produces more durable learning than re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006). Spreading practice over time beats cramming (Cepeda et al., 2006). Mixing problem types forces you to identify *which* skill a position needs, which is exactly what a real game demands (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007). Among chess players, the amount of serious solitary study is one of the strongest predictors of skill (Charness et al., 2005). The specific day counts and accuracy thresholds in this workbook are **coaching judgment**, not research findings.
:::

## Recovery plans for missed days

| You missed | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 day | Nothing special. Continue with the next day; do not double up. |
| 2–6 days | Do the next evaluation day first (re-test the week, update the journal), then continue where you stopped. |
| 7–13 days | Restart the current week from its first day. |
| 14+ days | Take the most recent stage test you passed again. If you score below 9/15, restart the current stage; otherwise restart the current week. |

Also: if you are ill, exhausted or upset, do the 15-minute option or rest. A skipped day is cheaper than a session played on tilt.

## Skill-specific remedial work

When the same mistake category appears three times in one week, add 10 minutes of remedial work to every session the following week:

| Category | Remedial work |
|---|---|
| hanging-piece | Re-read Chapter 4's safety scan; before every move in your next five games say "checks, captures, threats" for both sides. Re-solve Chapters 3–4. |
| missed-tactic | Re-solve the motif chapter (7–10) matching your misses; 10 extra exercises from your review queue daily. |
| missed-mate | Chapters 5 and 11: re-solve all mate exercises; set up and play out the basic mates against the engine. |
| king-safety | Chapter 6 (castle early), Chapter 21 (pawn shield, luft). |
| calculation | Chapters 14–15: solve every exercise without moving the pieces and write the whole line before checking. |
| opening | Chapters 12–13: replay your repertoire lines; compare your last three games' first 10 moves with the book. |
| endgame | Chapters 5, 18, 23: play the key positions against the engine until you win/hold them twice in a row. |
| missed-win / conversion | Chapter 16: play won positions (a piece up) against the engine until you convert three in a row. |
| positional | Chapters 19–20, 22: for each game, write one sentence on each side's worst piece and weakest pawn. |
| time-psychology | Chapter 27: one slower time control; a two-loss stop rule; a pre-game routine. |
