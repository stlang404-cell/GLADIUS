## The master decision checklist

Run this before every move in a slow game. With practice it takes seconds in quiet positions and you spend your real thinking time where it matters.

:::cheat Before every move
1. **What is my opponent threatening?** Ask of their last move: what does it attack, what did it stop defending, what line did it open?
2. **Are any pieces undefended?** Mine *and* theirs. Loose pieces are where tactics happen.
3. **Do I have forcing moves?** Every check, every capture, every threat — for both sides.
4. **What changes after my candidate move?** What does the moved piece stop guarding? Which squares and lines open?
5. **What is my opponent's strongest reply?** Look for their checks, captures and threats against the position *after* your move. Only then play it.
:::

:::cheat When ahead in material
1. Remove counterplay first: check the opponent's threats every move.
2. Trade pieces, not pawns (usually). Each trade makes your extra material count for more.
3. Keep your king safe; do not grab more material if it opens your king.
4. Activate your king in the endgame and create a passed pawn.
5. Before every move near the end: **check for stalemate.**
:::

:::cheat When behind in material
1. Seek activity: put pieces where they create threats.
2. Keep pieces on the board; avoid trades that simplify into a lost ending.
3. Look for tactical resources: checks, perpetual check, stalemate tricks, forks.
4. Set practical problems — make the opponent find good moves.
5. Do not resign out of frustration; resign only when you would be embarrassed to play on against a strong player.
:::

:::cheat When you do not know what to do
1. Which is my worst-placed piece? Improve it.
2. Which enemy piece or pawn is weakest? Attack it.
3. What does my opponent want? Prevent it, if it is cheap to do so.
4. Is there a pawn break that opens lines for my better-placed pieces?
5. Can I safely improve my king (endgame) or make it safer (middlegame)?
:::

:::coach How to use heuristics
Every procedure in this book is a guideline that is right most of the time. The concrete position always wins the argument: if a check, capture or threat changes the picture, calculate it.
:::
