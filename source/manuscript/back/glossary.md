Short, plain-English definitions of the chess terms used in this book. Where a term has a fuller explanation in a chapter, the definition here is only a reminder.

## A

**Active piece** — A piece that controls many squares, attacks targets or supports a plan. The opposite of a passive piece.

**Advantage** — A lasting plus for one side: extra material, a safer king, better pieces or a better pawn structure.

**Alapin Variation** — The Sicilian Defence line 1.e4 c5 2.c3, which prepares d2-d4. It is the book's choice for White against the Sicilian.

**Algebraic notation** — The standard way of writing chess moves, naming each square by a file letter (a–h) and a rank number (1–8), such as `Nf3` or `exd5`.

**Analysis board** — A digital board, found on most chess sites and apps, where you can set up positions, try moves and (optionally) switch on an engine.

**Annotation symbols** — Marks added to moves to judge them: `!` good, `!!` brilliant, `?` mistake, `??` blunder, `!?` interesting, `?!` dubious.

**Attack** — A threat to capture a piece, or a coordinated effort with several pieces against the enemy king.

## B

**Back-rank mate** — Checkmate by a rook or queen on the first or last rank, against a king trapped behind its own pawns.

**Backward pawn** — A pawn that has fallen behind its neighbours and cannot safely advance, often standing on a half-open file in front of an enemy piece.

**Bad bishop** — A bishop hemmed in by its own pawns fixed on squares of the same colour.

**Battery** — Two pieces lined up on the same line, such as a queen behind a bishop on a diagonal or two rooks doubled on a file.

**Bishop pair** — Having both bishops when the opponent does not. In open positions it is usually a small but real advantage.

**Blitz** — A fast time control, typically a few minutes per player for the whole game.

**Blockade** — Placing a piece, often a knight, directly in front of an enemy passed pawn to stop it.

**Blunder** — A serious mistake that loses material or the game, usually marked `??`.

**Blunder-check** — The habit of looking at your intended move from the opponent's side (checks, captures, threats) before you play it.

**Boden's mate** — A checkmate by two bishops on crossing diagonals, typically against a king castled on the queenside.

**Breakthrough** — A pawn sacrifice (or several) that clears the way for one pawn to promote.

**Building a bridge** — The winning method in the Lucena position: the rook goes to the fourth rank so that it can block the defending rook's checks.

## C

**Candidate moves** — The short list of moves you seriously consider before choosing one.

**Capture** — Removing an enemy piece from the board by moving onto its square.

**Castling** — A special move of king and rook that brings the king to safety and the rook towards the centre. It has strict conditions (see Chapter 1).

**Centre** — The squares d4, d5, e4 and e5, and more loosely the squares around them.

**Check** — A direct attack on the king. The player in check must get out of it at once.

**Checkmate** — A check that cannot be escaped. It ends the game.

**Checks, captures, threats** — The order of the safety scan: look first at all checks, then all captures, then all threats, for both sides.

**Chunk** — A familiar group of pieces that a player recognizes as one pattern rather than as separate pieces.

**Clearance** — A sacrifice or move that empties a square or line so that another piece can use it.

**Closed position** — A position with locked pawn chains and few open lines, where manoeuvring matters more than quick tactics.

**Connected passed pawns** — Two or more passed pawns on adjacent files that can protect each other.

**Conversion** — Turning an advantage into a win, usually by stopping counterplay and simplifying.

**Counterplay** — Active chances for the side that is worse, which force the stronger side to react.

**Counting** — Comparing the number of attackers and defenders of a square to see whether a capture there wins material.

**Critical moment** — A position where the choice you make now changes the course of the game, so that it deserves extra thinking time.

**Cut off (the king)** — Using a rook on a file or rank to keep the enemy king away from the important part of the board.

## D

**Decoy** — A sacrifice that lures an enemy piece, often the king, onto a bad square.

**Deflection** — Forcing an enemy piece away from a square or line it was defending.

**Deliberate practice** — Focused training aimed at specific weaknesses, with feedback, rather than simply playing for enjoyment.

**Desperado** — A piece that is lost anyway and captures as much as it can before it is taken.

**Development** — Bringing pieces from their starting squares into play.

**Discovered attack** — Moving one piece so that another piece behind it attacks something.

**Discovered check** — A discovered attack in which the uncovered piece gives check.

**Double attack** — One move that creates two threats at once. A fork is the most common type.

**Double check** — A check by two pieces at once. The king must move; nothing can block or capture both.

**Doubled pawns** — Two pawns of the same colour on the same file.

**Draw** — A game that ends with neither side winning, for example by agreement, stalemate, repetition, the fifty-move rule or insufficient material.

**Draw offer** — A proposal to end the game as a draw. Over the board it is made after making your move and before pressing your clock.

## E

**Elo rating** — A rating system, named after Arpad Elo, that estimates a player's strength from results against rated opponents.

**En passant** — A special pawn capture: a pawn that has just advanced two squares can be captured as if it had moved only one, but only on the very next move.

**Endgame** — The final phase of the game, with few pieces left, where king activity and passed pawns become decisive.

**Engine** — A computer program that plays and analyzes chess, such as Stockfish.

**Evaluation** — A judgment of who stands better. Engines express it as a number in pawns from White's point of view, such as +1.20.

**Exchange** — A trade of pieces. "Winning the exchange" means winning a rook for a bishop or knight.

## F

**FEN** — Forsyth–Edwards Notation: a one-line text description of a position, including whose move it is, which you can paste into any analysis board.

**Fianchetto** — Developing a bishop on b2, g2, b7 or g7 after moving the knight's pawn one square.

**Fifty-move rule** — A draw can be claimed when fifty moves by each side have been played with no capture and no pawn move.

**File** — A vertical column of the board, named a to h.

**First big swing** — In engine analysis, the first move where the evaluation changed by roughly a pawn or more.

**Flight square** — A square to which a king can escape, for example after a pawn move in front of a castled king. Also called luft.

**Fork** — One piece attacking two or more enemy pieces at the same time.

**Fortress** — A defensive set-up that the stronger side cannot break through, even with extra material.

## G

**Gambit** — An opening in which one side offers material, usually a pawn, for development or attack.

**Giuoco Piano** — The Italian Game with 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5. The slow version with c3 and d3 is called the Giuoco Pianissimo.

**Good bishop** — A bishop whose own pawns are mostly on squares of the opposite colour, so its diagonals are open.

**Greek gift** — The bishop sacrifice Bxh7+ (or ...Bxh2+) against a castled king, usually followed by a knight check on g5 and the queen joining the attack.

## H

**Half-open file** — A file with pawns of only one colour on it. It is open for the side without a pawn there.

**Hanging piece** — A piece that is attacked and not adequately defended.

**Hole** — A square that can no longer be controlled by your own pawns, which an enemy piece may occupy.

**Hope move** — A move played only because the opponent might miss a cheap threat, rather than because it improves the position.

## I

**Increment** — Time added to a player's clock after each move, for example the "+10" in a 15+10 time control.

**Initiative** — The ability to make threats that the opponent must answer, so that you control the course of the game.

**Insufficient material** — A position in which neither side can possibly checkmate, such as king against king, which is a draw.

**Interference** — Placing a piece on a line between an enemy piece and the square or piece it defends.

**Interleaving** — Mixing different types of problems in one practice session instead of practising one type at a time.

**Isolated pawn** — A pawn with no friendly pawns on the adjacent files.

## K

**Key squares** — In king and pawn endings, squares that guarantee promotion if the attacking king reaches them.

**King safety** — How well protected a king is from attack: pawn shelter, nearby defenders and the absence of open lines towards it.

**Kingside** — The half of the board on the king's side (files e to h).

## L

**Legal's mate** — A mating pattern in which a player sacrifices the queen to a pin and then mates with minor pieces, for example Nxe5, Bxf7+ and Nd5#.

**Loose piece** — An undefended piece, which is a natural target for double attacks.

**Lucena position** — The basic winning position in rook and pawn against rook, won by building a bridge.

**Luft** — German for "air": a flight square for the king, usually made by a pawn move such as h3 or ...h6.

## M

**Major piece** — A queen or rook.

**Mate pattern** — A familiar arrangement of pieces that gives checkmate, such as the back-rank mate or the smothered mate.

**Middlegame** — The phase after the opening, when most pieces are developed and plans and tactics dominate.

**Minor piece** — A bishop or knight.

**Minority attack** — Advancing a smaller group of pawns against a larger one in order to create weaknesses in the opponent's structure.

**Mistake categories** — The eleven labels this book uses to classify errors: hanging-piece, missed-tactic, missed-mate, king-safety, calculation, opening, endgame, missed-win, conversion, positional and time-psychology.

**Mistake journal** — A record of your significant mistakes with the position (FEN), the category, the cause and a rule for next time.

**Mobility** — The number of useful moves your pieces have.

## O

**Open file** — A file with no pawns on it, ideal for rooks.

**Open position** — A position with few pawns in the centre and many open lines, where piece activity and tactics matter most.

**Opening** — The first phase of the game, in which both sides develop their pieces and fight for the centre.

**Opening repertoire** — The set of openings you choose to play with each colour.

**Opposite-coloured bishops** — A position in which each side has one bishop and they move on different colours. In endings this often leads to a draw.

**Opposition** — In king endings, the situation in which the kings stand facing each other with one square between them and the side *not* to move holds the opposition.

**Outpost** — A square, usually in enemy territory, that cannot be attacked by enemy pawns and can be occupied by your piece, often a knight.

**Outside passed pawn** — A passed pawn far from the other pawns, which can drag the enemy king away.

**Overloading** — Giving an enemy piece more defensive duties than it can carry out at once.

**Overpressing** — Trying too hard to win an equal position, taking risks that make your own position worse.

## P

**Passed pawn** — A pawn with no enemy pawns in front of it on its own file or the adjacent files.

**Pawn break** — A pawn move that challenges the opponent's pawn chain and opens lines.

**Pawn chain** — A diagonal line of pawns protecting each other.

**Pawn majority** — More pawns than the opponent on one side of the board, which can create a passed pawn.

**Pawn structure** — The arrangement of the pawns, which shapes the plans for both sides.

**Performance rating** — A rating estimated from results or moves over a set of games. From a single game it means very little.

**Perpetual check** — A series of checks that the opponent cannot escape, leading to a draw.

**Philidor position** — A key drawing method in rook and pawn against rook: the defending rook stays on its third rank until the pawn advances, then checks from behind.

**Pin** — An attack on a piece that cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it. If the piece behind is the king, the pin is absolute.

**Plan** — A goal for the next several moves, based on the features of the position.

**Promotion** — A pawn reaching the last rank and becoming a queen, rook, bishop or knight.

**Prophylaxis** — A move that prevents the opponent's plan before it becomes a threat.

**Protected passed pawn** — A passed pawn defended by another pawn.

**Puzzle rating** — A rating for solving puzzles on a website. It measures something different from your playing rating.

## Q

**Queen's Gambit Declined** — The opening 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6, in which Black keeps the pawn on d5. It is the book's main defence for Black against 1.d4.

**Queenside** — The half of the board on the queen's side (files a to d).

**Quiet move** — A move that is not a check, capture or direct threat, often the hardest kind of move to find in a combination.

## R

**Rank** — A horizontal row of the board, numbered 1 to 8 from White's side.

**Rating** — A number estimating playing strength within one pool of players, such as one website or one federation.

**Removing the defender** — Capturing or driving away a piece that guards an important square or piece.

**Repetition** — A draw that can be claimed when the same position occurs three times with the same player to move and the same possible moves.

**Resignation** — Conceding the game before checkmate.

**Réti manoeuvre** — A diagonal king march that pursues two goals at once, named after the endgame study composed by Richard Réti.

**Retrieval practice** — Learning by recalling information from memory, for example by solving positions, rather than by re-reading.

**Rook lift** — Bringing a rook forward along a file, for example to the third rank, so that it can swing across to attack.

**Rule of the square** — A quick way to see whether a king can catch a passed pawn: draw the square from the pawn to its promotion rank and check whether the king can step into it.

## S

**Sacrifice** — Deliberately giving up material for an advantage such as an attack, a passed pawn or a draw.

**Safety scan** — The book's routine of checking all checks, captures and threats for both sides before moving.

**SAN** — Standard Algebraic Notation, the move format used in this book, such as `Nf3`, `exd5` or `O-O`.

**Scholar's mate** — The early mate on f7 with queen and bishop (for example Qh5, Bc4 and Qxf7#).

**Seventh rank** — The opponent's second rank. A rook there often attacks pawns and confines the enemy king.

**Simplification** — Trading pieces, usually to make a winning advantage easier to convert.

**Skewer** — An attack on a valuable piece that, when it moves, exposes a less valuable piece behind it.

**Smothered mate** — A knight checkmate against a king surrounded by its own pieces.

**Space** — Control of squares, especially in the opponent's half of the board.

**Spaced repetition** — Reviewing material at increasing intervals, such as 1, 3, 7 and 21 days.

**Stalemate** — A position in which the player to move is not in check but has no legal move. It is a draw.

**Swindle** — Saving or winning a bad position by setting practical problems that the opponent fails to solve.

## T

**Tempo** — A unit of time in chess: one move. Gaining a tempo means making a useful move while forcing the opponent to respond.

**Threat** — A move that will win something, or give mate, if it is not answered.

**Tilt** — Playing worse because you are upset, usually after a painful loss.

**Time control** — The amount of thinking time each player has, such as 15+10 (fifteen minutes plus ten seconds per move).

**Time trouble** — Having so little time left that you cannot think normally.

**Touch-move** — The over-the-board rule that a piece you deliberately touch must be moved (or, if it is an opponent's piece, captured) if that is legal.

**Trade** — Exchanging pieces of roughly equal value.

**Trapped piece** — A piece that cannot escape attack because all its squares are covered or blocked.

**Triangulation** — In king endings, losing a move by walking the king around a triangle of squares to put the opponent in zugzwang.

**Turning point** — A moment in a game where the evaluation or the character of the position changed decisively.

## U

**Underpromotion** — Promoting a pawn to a rook, bishop or knight instead of a queen, usually to avoid stalemate or to give check.

## W

**Weak square** — A square that cannot be defended by pawns and can be used by enemy pieces.

**Weakness list** — A ranking of your mistake categories by how often they occur and how much they cost you.

**Wrong-coloured bishop** — A bishop that does not control the promotion square of its own rook pawn, so that the defending king in the corner holds the draw.

## X

**X-ray** — An attack or defence by a piece through another piece on the same line.

## Z

**Zugzwang** — A position in which any move worsens your position, so that you would prefer to pass.

**Zwischenzug** — German for "in-between move": an unexpected move, often a check or threat, played before the expected recapture.
